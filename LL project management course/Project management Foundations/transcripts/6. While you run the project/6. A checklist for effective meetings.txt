A Checklist for Effective Meetings
Effective meetings are so beneficial. They can save time for all attendees (reducing labor costs at the same time) and produce better outcomes. But most meetings are effective by accident. Here are ground rules and approaches to make sure your meetings are productive and efficient. 

Set end time 10 minutes before the hour or half hour, to allow time between meetings for transit time, short errands, etc. (Hold 20-minute, 50-minute, or 80-minute meetings.)
Start and stop meetings on time.
Publish an agenda—and follow it.
Ensure the meeting objective is universally understood and agreed on.
Keep to the time allotted for each agenda item.
Notify presenters in advance.
Everyone in attendance should have a clear purpose for being at the meeting.
Uninvite people who do not need to be in the meeting.
Designate defined roles.
Facilitator and a backup
Timekeeper
Notetaker
Come prepared.
Leave job titles at the door when brainstorming.
One person speaks at a time—no side conversations.
Respect each other’s comments and opinions.
Assign an owner and due date to every action and decision.
No more than 2 hours without taking a break. (90 minutes is better.)
Make sure any technical tools used are appropriate and used appropriately by all participants.
Check in with everyone regularly, especially remote participants.
Keep people’s involvement balanced.
Include written responses to questions to balance extroverts and introverts.
Manage dominant participants.
Ask questions to anyone who seems to be quiet.
Use open-ended questions to elicit more information.
Distribute meeting minutes to all participants and relevant stakeholders.
Follow these guidelines and your meetings will be to the point and productive. Plus, you’ll become everyone’s favorite project manager (or manager or team member).
